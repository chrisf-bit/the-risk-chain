# Safeguarding video scripts (SG-F3 first; F1/F2/F4 to follow)

**For:** avatar / talking-head production · **From:** Chris / Rapid Learn
**Companion to:** the choking `F1`/`F2-F4` video docs. **Same production rules carry across.**

These cover the first safeguarding build, **SG-F3 "She Tells You at Ten Past Four"** (Nadia,
Sandhill Road, late shift). SG-F1, SG-F2 and SG-F4 scripts get added here as those scenarios
are built.

**Personas (keep consistent with the choking programme):**
- **EDUCATOR** - calm practice educator / assessor. One recurring face. Used for the welcome
  (intro) and every debrief segment, across every scenario. Match the existing EDUCATOR framing,
  wardrobe, lighting and background exactly.
- **COLLEAGUE** - a diegetic handover voice. SG-F3 is lone-worked with nobody handing over in the
  room, so the handover is written for the **EDUCATOR in second person**.

**Universal production notes (unchanged):**
- Every clip is **self-contained**. Never "as I said earlier / next"; order can change per run.
- Neutral, unhurried delivery. The register is "nothing dramatic", especially on any heavy beat.
- Captions supplied as an inline base64 VTT track (accessibility + SCORM), same as the choking build.
- **No em dashes** in any spoken line. Durations are targets; shorter is better.
- 16:9, 1920x1080. Match the existing talking-head framing so the landing / handover / debrief
  video tiles are consistent across both programmes.

## AVATAR SAFETY IS A HARD CONSTRAINT (and tighter here than choking)

Safeguarding is a **legal** subject as well as a clinical-adjacent one, so Synthesia's medical
policy is joined by the risk of an avatar appearing to give **legal instruction**. The rule for
every clip below:

- The **avatar speaks only the human, non-clinical, non-legal framing** of the shift: the setting,
  the pressure, the people, and the "no hints, your choices decide it" spine.
- **Everything statutory or specific stays on screen, never in the avatar's mouth:** the Care Act
  s42 test, the MCA, DBS referral, "call 999" / "call the police", the category names (sexual
  abuse, coercive control, financial abuse), referral and notification duties, and anything about
  what was or was not a crime. These already live in the app as on-screen text (landing copy, fact
  tiles, records, decision beats, the debrief), which is not avatar-generated.
- If a clip is flagged, move the offending line into on-screen copy and keep the spoken version
  reflective and general. When drafting new copy, **match the wording of a clip that already
  passed** rather than inventing near-equivalents (the lesson from the choking build).

## 0. SG-F3 INTRO · EDUCATOR · ~20s
*Plays inline on the SG-F3 landing and tees the shift up. Human framing only; the detail about
Nadia, the disclosure themes and the six o'clock deadline sits in the on-screen landing copy and
fact tiles. Keep the shared spine consistent with the choking intros.*

> Welcome to She Tells You at Ten Past Four. You are on a late shift, on your own, with four people
> to support. In the next couple of hours one of them is going to trust you with something, and a
> clock is already running toward the end of your shift.
>
> There are no hints, and no single right answer. What happens is whatever your choices make happen.
>
> What matters most here is not what you work out. It is what you do with what you are told. When
> you are ready, begin your shift.

*Moved to on-screen copy (already in the landing text): Nadia is 29 and autistic, needs processing
time, her uncle collects her at six, her boyfriend holds her bank card and phone.*

## 0b. CONTENT NOTICE · on-screen text (no video)
*Rendered before the shift on the "Before you begin" screen (the app's notice gate), not spoken.
No recording needed. This is a build requirement, not optional (spec s17.1). Themes named without
spoiling the scenario:*

> This scenario involves a disclosure that touches on sexual abuse and on coercive control within a
> relationship. The disclosure is written to be ordinary and non-explicit. Nothing is depicted. You
> can stop at any point. Opting out carries no score penalty and is not flagged to your employer.

## 1. SG-F3 HANDOVER · EDUCATOR, second person · ~34s
*Plays in the media band on the handover screen. Setting: Sandhill Road, a shared supported living
house, late shift, lone worker. The clock to the six o'clock collection starts when the learner
goes through to the kitchen.*

> It is ten past four, and the house is yours for the rest of the shift. Three of the people here
> are settled for now. The fourth has been upstairs since lunchtime and has just come down.
>
> Her bag is by the door. Someone is collecting her at six, the way they do most Fridays. That is
> the shape of the next couple of hours, and the clock is already running.
>
> Before you go through, take a proper look at what the house holds about the people you are
> supporting. The thing that matters today may not be the thing that is flagged. When you are
> ready, go to the kitchen.

*Everything about the uncle, the boyfriend, the disclosure and the statutory routes is carried by
the on-screen handover text and the records, not spoken.*

## 2. SG-F3 DEBRIEF · one clip per outcome · EDUCATOR
*One self-contained clip per outcome path (A protected / B delayed / C harm continued). The debrief
has a single video slot and plays the clip matching the run's outcome, so there is no stitching.
Each clip is the human framing plus the verdict, then hands to the on-screen breakdown (the two
critical-control cards, the six domain bars, "what Nadia wanted", captured-this-run, the transfer
question). Keep the C cut sombre and unhurried, no music sting. Reflective, never instructional:
no category names, no "call 999", no legal duties spoken.*

**A · Protected · ~28s**
> Nothing dramatic happened, and that is the point. Someone trusted you with something difficult,
> and you took it seriously without taking it over. You kept her safe, you kept her trust, and you
> made sure it reached the person whose job it is to help, before your shift ended. The screen
> shows how each part of your practice held up, and the two things this shift really turned on.

**B · Protection delayed · ~30s**
> You got there. But it came later and closer than it should have, and the screen shows where the
> time went. Recognising that something is wrong and putting a poor first instinct right is real
> practice, and it counts. What we look at now is the moment the response started to lag behind the
> risk, because on a different day that gap is the whole story.

**C · Harm continued · ~32s · sombre, unhurried, no music sting**
> Take a moment before we go on. By the end of this shift, what you were trusted with had not
> reached anyone who could act on it, and you have seen what followed. This was not one mistake, and
> it is not about blame. It was a handful of choices across one afternoon, and any one of them, made
> differently, would have changed the ending. The screen walks through each of them. Sit with it,
> because the whole of this training exists to keep a shift from reaching that point.

### 2b. What is on-screen, not spoken (nothing is lost)
Rendered in the debrief itself, so no clip and no stitching:
- **The two critical controls** (CC1 confidentiality / concern withheld, CC2 the collection or
  tipping off) shown held or tripped, each with its explanation. CC3 (evidence / contamination)
  surfaces when it was tripped and whether it was recovered.
- **The six frontline domain bars** (recognition, response and rights, recording, reporting and
  escalation, capacity and consent, personal courage).
- **"What Nadia wanted to happen"** and whether the learner asked and recorded it (the person's
  voice close, the most-missed point).
- **The aftermath sequence** (72 hours / three months, with the learner's own record read back) on
  the B and C paths, reached before the debrief.
- **Captured this run** chips, and the "an unsubstantiated concern reported in good faith is a
  correct act" line.

## Wellbeing / safe-use note
Content notice before the scenario, a second notice before the aftermath, a no-penalty opt-out,
emotional-impact reflection in the debrief, and a signposted support route on the closing screen are
all **build requirements** (spec s17.1), already wired into the app. The spoken clips must never
undercut them with drama. All statutory and emergency wording is Emma's (SME) to confirm before
recording, and is carried on screen, not spoken.
